# Findings

**Classification: Portfolio scenario.** These are illustrative findings constructed for this exercise to demonstrate the analysis method, not real research output.

## Synthesized Findings

**Existing habits already cover most of the stated need.**
Most employees already take their own notes or rely on a designated note-taker for meetings where follow-up actually matters (decision-making meetings). The complaint about "not remembering what happened" was strongest for low-stakes meetings, where the actual cost of not having a summary is also low.

**The failures that do occur are about ownership, not memory.**
When something did get dropped, the more common cause was an action item with no clear owner or deadline — not that nobody could recall the meeting happened. An AI summary would restate the ambiguity, not resolve it.

**Trust in AI-generated summaries is conditional.**
Employees said they'd still want to skim a summary against their own notes for anything consequential, particularly for meetings involving sensitive topics (performance discussions, compensation, legal matters) — meaning the feature wouldn't fully replace the behavior it's meant to reduce.

**Existing alternatives are already partially adopted.**
Some teams already use calendar-integrated recording or transcription tools for meetings where a record matters. The gap is narrower than the original proposal assumed.

## What This Changes

The proposal assumed a broad, unmet need. The synthesized findings point to a narrower, already partially served need, concentrated in a subset of meeting types — which materially changes the [Opportunity Sizing](../opportunity-sizing/opportunity.md) and the cost/value comparison in the [Decision Memo](../decision-memo/decision.md).
