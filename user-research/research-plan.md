# User Research Plan

**Classification: Portfolio scenario — describes the research approach that would be used; no research was actually conducted.**

## Objective

Determine whether employees are genuinely underserved by existing tools and habits around meeting notes and follow-up, or whether the perceived need is broader and shallower than the proposal assumes.

## Questions to Answer

1. How do employees currently capture and share what happened in a meeting?
2. Where do existing methods (manual notes, calendar recordings, existing transcription tools) actually fail them?
3. How often does the *lack* of a meeting summary cause a real, costly outcome — a missed action item, a repeated decision, a dropped commitment — versus how often it's a minor inconvenience?
4. Would employees trust an AI-generated summary of a meeting they attended, or would they still feel the need to verify it against their own notes?

## Method (As Would Be Applied)

- Survey a cross-section of roles and meeting types (status meetings, decision-making meetings, one-on-ones) on current note-taking and follow-up habits.
- Interview a smaller group specifically about a recent instance where lack of a summary caused a real problem, to separate genuine gaps from general complaints about "too many meetings."
- Review existing tool usage data (e.g., adoption of any transcription or note tools already available) as a proxy for demonstrated, not just stated, need.

## Why This Method

Stated preference ("I'd like an AI summary") is a weak signal on its own — almost everyone would say yes to a feature that sounds convenient. Asking about a specific past incident and reviewing existing tool usage gets closer to revealed need, not just a generically appealing idea.
