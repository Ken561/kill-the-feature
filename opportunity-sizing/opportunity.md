# Opportunity Sizing

**Classification: Portfolio scenario.** Ranges are illustrative and directional, used to demonstrate the sizing method — not real financial estimates.

## Framing the Opportunity

The proposal implicitly sizes the opportunity as "every meeting, every employee" — the broadest possible framing. Based on the [Findings](../user-research/findings.md), the actual addressable opportunity is narrower:

| Segment | Addressable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Low-stakes status meetings | Low value | Already low cost when summary is missing; existing habits are adequate |
| Decision-making meetings | Medium value | Real cost when action items are dropped, but the root cause is unclear ownership, not lack of a summary |
| Sensitive meetings (HR, legal, compensation) | Not addressable / higher risk | Employees explicitly want less automated recording here, not more, and privacy risk is highest |
| Teams already using transcription tools | Already served | Incremental value from a new feature is low — this segment already has a working alternative |

## Incremental Value Estimate

Given the above, the realistic addressable opportunity is concentrated in **decision-making meetings without an existing transcription habit** — a real but narrower slice than "every meeting" implies. Estimated incremental value: **medium**, not the "clear productivity win" framing in the original proposal.

## Why Sizing Narrower Matters

A broad framing ("every meeting, every employee") makes almost any feature look valuable by inflating the addressable population. Sizing the opportunity against where the [Findings](../user-research/findings.md) show a real, unserved gap — not against the total possible user base — is what surfaces the medium-value, not high-value, conclusion driving this decision.
